<h1 style="color: #ab47bc;">📑 Unit 6 — Make Sense of It</h1>

La interpretación, procesamiento y transmisión de la información contenida en correos electrónicos corporativos, artículos de divulgación laboral y manuales técnicos de usuario en lengua inglesa exige dominar las pautas de correspondencia digital, las estrictas reglas sintácticas del estilo indirecto (*Reported Speech*) para enunciados, preguntas y mandatos, y la lectura metódica de especificaciones de hardware y software.

---

<h2 style="color: #29b6f6;">6.1. Understanding Emails</h2>

La correspondencia electrónica profesional en entornos tecnológicos se rige por un diseño estandarizado orientado a la claridad, la concisión y la trazabilidad operativa:

### Estructura Canónica del Correo Electrónico Profesional
* **Subject Line (Línea de Asunto):** Debe encapsular el propósito central del mensaje en pocas palabras, incorporando etiquetas de clasificación si procede (*"Reminder: Team Training Session", "Notice: System Maintenance", "Urgent: Firewall Update"*).
* **Greetings & Salutations (Encabezamientos):**
    * *Formal:* *"Dear Sir / Madam", "Dear Mr. / Ms. [Surname]"*.
    * *Semiformal / Interno:* *"Hi Team", "Good morning [Name]"*.
* **Core Content / Body Paragraphs (Cuerpo del Mensaje):** Exposición estructurada en párrafos breves donde se detalla el motivo de la comunicación, fechas clave, horas de ventanas de mantenimiento, instrucciones de actuación y solicitudes explícitas de confirmación o respuesta (*RSVP*).
* **Sign-off / Closings (Cierres y Despedidas):**
    * *Formal:* *"Sincerely", "Yours faithfully"*.
    * *Semiformal:* *"Best regards", "Kind regards", "Looking forward to hearing from you"*.

<h2 style="color: #29b6f6;">6.2. Reported Speech – Reglas de Transformación Gramatical (Statements)</h2>

El **Reported Speech** (*Estilo Indirecto*) se utiliza para transmitir el contenido de lo que otra persona dijo sin emplear sus palabras textuales (*Direct Speech*). Cuando el verbo introductorio (*reporting verb*, como *said* o *told*) está en pasado, se produce obligatoriamente una regresión temporal en los verbos de la subordinada conocida como **Backshift of Tenses** (*salto atrás en el tiempo verbal*).

### Matriz de Transformación de Tiempos Verbales (*Backshift*)

| Tiempo en Direct Speech (Estilo Directo) | Tiempo en Reported Speech (Estilo Indirecto) | Ejemplo Práctico en Entorno Técnico |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present Simple**<br>*"I like the new software"* | **Past Simple**<br>*He said (that) he liked the new software.* | *Direct:* "The server runs slowly."<br>*Reported:* He said that the server ran slowly. |
| **Present Continuous**<br>*"I am updating the OS"* | **Past Continuous**<br>*She said (that) she was updating the OS.* | *Direct:* "We are testing the patch."<br>*Reported:* They said they were testing the patch. |
| **Past Simple**<br>*"I installed the driver"* | **Past Perfect Simple**<br>*He said (that) he had installed the driver.* | *Direct:* "The power cut off yesterday."<br>*Reported:* She said the power had cut off the previous day. |
| **Present Perfect Simple**<br>*"I've fixed the bug"* | **Past Perfect Simple**<br>*She said (that) she had fixed the bug.* | *Direct:* "I've backed up the data."<br>*Reported:* He said he had backed up the data. |
| **Future Simple (`will`)**<br>*"I will reboot the router"* | **`Would` + Infinitivo**<br>*He said (that) he would reboot the router.* | *Direct:* "We will deploy the update."<br>*Reported:* They said they would deploy the update. |
| **`Can`**<br>*"Can you help me?"* | **`Could`**<br>*She asked if we could help her.* | *Direct:* "Can the system handle this load?"<br>*Reported:* He asked if the system could handle that load. |
| **`May`**<br>*"I may be late"* | **`Might`**<br>*Jack said (that) he might be late.* | *Direct:* "The link may fail."<br>*Reported:* The operator said the link might fail. |
| **`Must` / `Have to`**<br>*"I must upgrade"* | **`Had to`**<br>*She said (that) she had to upgrade.* | *Direct:* "We must replace the PSU."<br>*Reported:* The lead engineer said they had to replace the PSU. |

---

### Verbos Modales Invariables (Sin Backshift)
Los verbos modales de grado lógico avanzado y pasado no experimentan modificación al pasar a estilo indirecto: **`Past Perfect`**, **`Could`**, **`Might`**, **`Should`** y **`Mustn't`** permanecen inalterados.

### Conversión de Indicadores Espacio-Temporales y Demostrativos

| Estilo Directo (*Direct Speech*) | Estilo Indirecto (*Reported Speech*) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **this / these** | **that / those** |
| **here** | **there** |
| **now** | **then** |
| **today / tonight** | **that day / that night** |
| **yesterday** | **the day before / the previous day** |
| **tomorrow** | **the next day / the following day** |
| **next week / month** | **the following week / month** |
| **last week / month** | **the week / month before (the previous week/month)** |
| **ago** | **before** |

<h2 style="color: #29b6f6;">6.3. Reported Questions, Commands, Requests & Suggestions</h2>

### 1. Reported Questions (Preguntas Indirectas)
Al transformar una pregunta directa a estilo indirecto, se eliminan los signos de interrogación, desaparecen los auxiliares de soporte interrogativo (**`do`**, **`does`**, **`did`**) y la estructura adopta obligatoriamente el orden declarativo afirmativo (**Sujeto + Verbo**):
* **Yes/No Questions:** Se introduce la subordinada mediante las conjunciones **`if`** o **`whether`** ("si"):  
  *Direct:* `"Do you have administrative rights?"*  
  *Reported:* The technician asked **if** I **had** administrative rights.
* **Wh- Questions:** Se conserva la partícula interrogativa original (*who, what, where, when, why, how*):  
  *Direct:* `"Where do you store the system backups?"*  
  *Reported:* She asked me **where** we **stored** the system backups.

---

### 2. Reported Commands and Requests (Órdenes y Peticiones)
Se estructuran empleando los verbos introductorios **`tell`** (órdenes) o **`ask`** (peticiones) seguidos del **objeto de persona** y la partícula de infinitivo **`(not) to`**:
* **Estructura:** Sujeto + *told / asked* + Objeto + `(not) to` + Infinitivo.
* *Direct Command:* `"Do not disconnect the power cable during the process."*  
* *Reported Command:* The supervisor **asked** the operator **not to disconnect** the power cable during the process.

---

### 3. Reported Suggestions (Sugerencias)
El verbo **`suggest`** admite dos fórmulas sintácticas válidas en estilo indirecto, quedando **estrictamente prohibido** el uso de infinitivo con *to* (*suggest to go* es incorrecto):
1. *Suggest* + `(that)` + Sujeto + Verbo en infinitivo sin *to* (o presente simple):  
   *“Let's run a diagnostic script.”* $\rightarrow$ He **suggested (that) we run** a diagnostic script.
2. *Suggest* + Verbo en gerundio (`-ing`):  
   *“Let's run a diagnostic script.”* $\rightarrow$ He **suggested running** a diagnostic script.

<h2 style="color: #29b6f6;">6.4. Understanding Instructions in Manuals & Technical Documentation</h2>

La lectura comprensiva de manuales de usuario y guías técnicas de hardware y software requiere identificar la segmentación modular del documento:

* **Headings & Structure (Secciones y Encabezados):** Localización rápida mediante bloques estandarizados (*Unpack components, Assembly steps, Software Setup, Troubleshooting guides, Preventive Maintenance*).
* **Iconografía de Seguridad y Alertas:** Interpretación de avisos de advertencia para prevenir daños materiales o personales (*"Caution: Risk of electrostatic discharge", "Avoid over-tightening screws"*).
* **Rutas de Navegación de Software:** Extracción de secuencias lógicas de configuración en interfaces gráficas (*"Go to Settings > System > Recovery > Reset this PC"*).

<h2 style="color: #29b6f6;">6.5. Understanding Articles (Comprensión de Artículos Corporativos)</h2>

La lectura de artículos de divulgación empresarial e informática sobre gestión de personal y entornos laborales aborda temáticas recurrentes:

* **Temáticas Centrales:** Implantación del trabajo híbrido (*hybrid work*), prevención del desgaste profesional (*burnout*), estrategias de retención de talento (*retaining top talent*), ciberseguridad corporativa y optimización de canales de comunicación interna.
* **Análisis Argumentativo:** Identificación de la tesis principal del autor, análisis de los beneficios de bienestar laboral (*wellness benefits*) propuestos y evaluación de los datos empíricos que respaldan las conclusiones del artículo.

<h2 style="color: #29b6f6;">🎯 Practical Scenarios & Technical Application</h2>

!!! example "Scenario 1: Helpdesk Incident Escalation in Reported Speech"
    **Context:** A tier-1 helpdesk agent transmits a user's critical issue to the senior systems administrator:  
    * *"User's direct statement: **'The database server crashed an hour ago, and I haven't saved my progress.'**  
    Agent's report to the admin: The user reported that the database server **had crashed** an hour **before** and that he **hadn't saved** his progress."*  
    * **Linguistic breakdown:** Backshift application from Past Simple (*crashed*) to Past Perfect Simple (*had crashed*); time expression adjustment (*ago* $\rightarrow$ *before*); pronoun adjustment (*I* $\rightarrow$ *he*).

!!! example "Scenario 2: Hardware Assembly Manual Instructions"
    **Context:** Excerpt from an ergonomic office chair assembly guide:  
    * *"First, unpack all components and verify the parts list. Install the gas lift cylinder into the center of the wheelbase. Next, attach the backrest to the seat mechanism using the provided Allen key, and **avoid over-tightening** the securing screws. Finally, test the height adjustment lever to ensure smooth operation."*  
    * **Linguistic breakdown:** Imperative verbs for technical assembly (*unpack, verify, install, attach, test*); sequence markers (*First, Next, Finally*); technical warning (*avoid over-tightening*).

<h2 style="color: #29b6f6;">📌 Technical Appendix: Exam Keys (Claves PAC)</h2>

!!! danger "Critical Grammar & Linguistic Rules for Assessment"
    1. **Strict Distinction Between *Say* and *Tell* (Classic Exam Trap):**
        * **`Say`**: Does **not** take a direct personal object immediately after. Used alone or with *that* (*"He said that he was busy"*). ❌ *He said me that...* is grammatically incorrect.
        * **`Tell`**: Requires **mandatory** personal indirect object pronouns (*me, him, her, us, them, the team*) (*"He told me that he was busy"*). ❌ *He told that he was busy* is incorrect.
    2. **Auxiliary Elimination in Reported Questions:**
        * When transforming direct questions in Present Simple or Past Simple into indirect speech, the interrogative auxiliary verbs (**`do`**, **`does`**, **`did`**) **must be completely eliminated**, and the word order changes to declarative (**Subject + Verb**):
            * *Direct:* `"Where do you store the logs?"*
            * *Reported:* ✔️ ***They asked me where I stored the logs.*** (NUNCA ❌ *...where did I store...*).
    3. **Negative Commands Syntax (*Not + To + Infinitive*):**
        * The negative particle *not* must **always** precede the infinitive with *to*:
            * ❌ *She asked me to not restart the router.* / ❌ *She asked me don't restart.*
            * ✔️ ***She asked me not to restart the router.***
    4. **Verbs of Suggestion (*Suggest* Grammar):**
        * The verb **`suggest`** can **never** be followed by an infinitive with *to*:
            * ❌ *He suggested to upgrade the system.*
            * ✔️ ***He suggested upgrading the system.*** O ✔️ ***He suggested that we upgrade the system.***
    5. **Invariable Modals in Indirect Speech:**
        * Remember that *must* shifts to *had to*, but **`mustn't`**, **`should`**, **`could`**, and **`might`** remain **completely unchanged** in reported speech.
