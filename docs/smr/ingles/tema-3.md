<h1 style="color: #ab47bc;">💬 Unit 3 — Express Yourself</h1>

El dominio de la comunicación profesional en entornos internacionales de soporte y administración de sistemas exige estructurar con precisión todas las formas de expresión de futuro, coordinar diálogos laborales formales e informales, aplicar de forma rigurosa la sintaxis de adjetivos y adverbios temporales (*yet, still, already, for, since*), y cohesionar discursos técnicos mediante conectores lógicos de contraste, adición, causa y finalidad.

---

<h2 style="color: #29b6f6;">3.1. Greetings, Welcoming, and Saying Goodbye</h2>

La interacción oral en entornos corporativos requiere modular el registro lingüístico (formal, neutro o informal) según la jerarquía del interlocutor, el contexto del mensaje y la relación laboral.

### Repertorio Funcional de Fórmulas Conversacionales

| Función Comunicativa | Registro Formal / Profesional | Registro Informal / Cotidiano |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Saludos de Apertura (*Greetings*)** | *Good morning / Good afternoon.*<br>*It is a pleasure to meet you.*<br>*How do you do?* (Fórmula protocolaria) | *Hi! / Hello!*<br>*Hey, what's up?*<br>*Long time no see!* |
| **Fórmulas de Bienvenida (*Welcoming*)** | *Welcome to our facilities.*<br>*Please make yourself comfortable.*<br>*Feel free to ask me any questions regarding the network.* | *Glad to have you here!*<br>*Make yourself at home.*<br>*Come on in!* |
| **Mantenimiento del Diálogo (*Small Talk*)** | *How is your onboarding process going?*<br>*Are you finding the documentation clear?*<br>*Do you feel you have been able to settle in?* | *How is your day going so far?*<br>*Looking forward to catching up later.*<br>*How are things with the new project?* |
| **Fórmulas de Cierre (*Farewells / Goodbyes*)** | *It was a pleasure meeting you.*<br>*Have a productive day.*<br>*Goodbye.* | *See you later!*<br>*Take care!*<br>*Bye for now / Catch you soon.*<br>*I should head home now.* |

<h2 style="color: #29b6f6;">3.2. Future Time: Will & Going to</h2>

El sistema de expresión de futuro en inglés no se articula mediante una desinencia verbal única, sino a través de operadores modales, perífrasis verbales y tiempos de presente condicionados por la certeza, la intencionalidad o el origen de la predicción.

### Contraste Fundamental: Will vs. Be Going to

```text
  FUTURE SIMPLE (WILL)                     BE GOING TO
  [Sujeto + will + Infinitivo]             [Sujeto + am/is/are + going to + Infinitivo]
         │                                        │
         ├─ Decisión espontánea en el habla       ├─ Plan / Intención meditada previa
         ├─ Predicción basada en opinión          ├─ Predicción con evidencia física presente
         └─ Promesas, ofertas y rechazos          └─ Acción en marcha orientada al futuro
```

* **Future Simple (`will` + infinitivo):**
    * *Decisión espontánea inmediata:* *"The switch port is inactive; I'll connect to the console now."*
    * *Predicción subjetiva u opinión:* *"I think optical fiber will replace all copper links soon."*
    * *Promesas y compromisos:* *"We will restore the backup before 18:00, I promise."*
* **Be going to (`am/is/are` + `going to` + infinitivo):**
    * *Plan o intención deliberada previa:* *"The IT department is going to deploy Windows 11 next month."*
    * *Predicción respaldada por evidencia física observable:* *"Look at the CPU temperature spike; the server is going to crash."*

---

### Expresión de Futuro mediante Tiempos de Presente
* **Present Continuous con valor de futuro:** Expresa **citas o acuerdos cerrados (*arrangements*)** con terceras personas o entidades, donde se han fijado fecha y hora:  
  *The network architect **is meeting** the hardware vendor on Thursday morning.*
* **Present Simple con valor de futuro:** Se reserva de forma estricta para **horarios oficiales, programaciones públicas e itinerarios (*timetables*)**:  
  *The maintenance window **starts** at 23:00 and **closes** at 05:00.*

---

### Estructuras Perifrásticas Avanzadas de Futuro

| Estructura Perifrástica | Patrón Sintáctico | Significado Funcional | Ejemplo en Entorno Técnico |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Be due to** | Sujeto + `be due to` + Infinitivo | Suceso previsto u oficial con hora o fecha prefijada. | *The automated backup is due to run at midnight.* |
| **Be about to** | Sujeto + `be about to` + Infinitivo | Suceso inminente a punto de ocurrir en segundos o minutos. | *Save your current work; the power is about to cut off.* |
| **Be to** | Sujeto + `be to` + Infinitivo | Órdenes formales, instrucciones institucionales o protocolos. | *All employees are to change their credentials before Friday.* |

<h2 style="color: #29b6f6;">3.3. Adverbs & Adjectives. Yet, Still, Already, For & Since</h2>

### Adjetivos frente a Adverbios
* **Adjetivos (*Adjectives*):** Modifican y describen sustantivos (*a critical error, an efficient script*). En inglés son **invariables** (no admiten marca de género ni de plural: *a fast computer / four fast computers*).
* **Adverbios de Modo (*Adverbs of Manner*):** Modifican verbos, adjetivos u otros adverbios (*runs smoothly, extremely fast*). Se forman por regla general añadiendo el sufijo **`-ly`** a la raíz del adjetivo:
    * *careful* $\rightarrow$ *carefully*
    * *quick* $\rightarrow$ *quickly*
    * *automatic* $\rightarrow$ *automatically*
* **Casos Irregulares y Excepciones:**
    * *good* (adjetivo) $\rightarrow$ **`well`** (adverbio): *He is a good technician; he configures servers well.*
    * *fast, hard, late* mantienen la misma forma como adjetivo y adverbio (*a fast link / runs fast*).
    * Palabras terminadas en `-ly` que son **adjetivos**, no adverbios: *friendly, costly, daily, weekly*.

#### Orden Canónico de Adjetivos ante Sustantivo
Cuando concurren múltiples adjetivos atribuidos a un mismo nombre, se ordenan según la siguiente jerarquía:  
**Opinión $\rightarrow$ Tamaño $\rightarrow$ Forma $\rightarrow$ Edad/Antigüedad $\rightarrow$ Color $\rightarrow$ Origen $\rightarrow$ Material $\rightarrow$ Propósito + Sustantivo**  
*An efficient (opinión) small (tamaño) rectangular (forma) new (edad) black (color) Taiwanese (origen) metallic (material) storage (propósito) device.*

---

### Adverbios Temporales de Relación: Already, Still, Yet, For, Since

| Adverbio Temporal | Polaridad y Posición Típica | Valor Semántico | Ejemplo en Sistemas |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Already** | Oraciones afirmativas; entre el auxiliar y el verbo principal. | Indica que una acción ha concluido **antes de lo previsto** o con anterioridad al momento actual. | *The sysadmin has **already** applied the security patch.* |
| **Still** | Oraciones afirmativas o negativas; antes del verbo principal o del auxiliar negativo. | Señala que una **situación continuada persiste** en el presente sin resolverse. | *The network link is **still** down.*<br>*We **still** haven't received the replacement RAM.* |
| **Yet** | Oraciones negativas e interrogativas; posicionado **al final de la cláusula**. | Expresa que un suceso esperado **aún no ha acontecido** o interroga sobre su compleción. | *The database migration hasn't finished **yet**.*<br>*Have you verified the integrity checksum **yet**?* |
| **For** | Con expresiones de intervalo temporal medible. | Cuantifica la **duración total** de una actividad continuada. | *The server has been running **for** 120 days.* |
| **Since** | Con puntos cronológicos concretos de referencia. | Marca el **punto de inicio específico** en el tiempo de un proceso en curso. | *The database has been operational **since** last Monday.* |

<h2 style="color: #29b6f6;">3.4. Starting, Maintaining, and Ending Conversations</h2>

En las interacciones técnicas de soporte al usuario (*Helpdesk*), estructurar el flujo de conversación de forma organizada transmite profesionalidad y facilita el triaje de incidencias:

1. **Apertura y Sondeo Inicial:**  
   *"IT Support, this is Luis speaking. How can I assist you with your workstation today?"*
2. **Mantenimiento y Comprobación de Diagnóstico:**  
   *"I see your point. While I check the server connectivity, could you confirm if the error reproduces on other terminals?"*
3. **Validación y Conclusión del Soporte:**  
   *"The patch has been deployed and the service is responding correctly. Is there anything else I can resolve for you before closing this ticket?"*

<h2 style="color: #29b6f6;">3.5. Connectors (Linkers)</h2>

Los **conectores discursivos** enlazan proposiciones lógicas e introducen relaciones de dependencia funcional en informes técnicos y comunicaciones corporativas.

### Clasificación Funcional de Conectores

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                  MATRIZ FUNCIONAL DE CONECTORES LÓGICOS                 |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------------+
| 1. CONTRASTE      | however, nevertheless, although, despite, whereas   |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------------+
| 2. ADICIÓN        | furthermore, moreover, in addition, besides, also   |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------------+
| 3. CAUSA Y EFECTO | therefore, consequently, as a result, because (of)  |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------------+
| 4. FINALIDAD      | in order to, so as to, so that (+ cláusula), for    |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------------+
| 5. CONCLUSIÓN     | in conclusion, to sum up, in short, overall         |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------------------------+
```

#### 1. Conectores de Contraste (*Contrast*)
* **However / Nevertheless / Nonetheless:** Seguidos de coma al inicio de oración o entre puntos y comas (*The drive failed; however, no data was lost*).
* **Although / Even though / Though:** Introducen una **oración completa subordinada** con sujeto y verbo (*Although the link was saturated, the QoS prioritized VoIP traffic*).
* **Despite / In spite of:** Rigen de forma obligatoria un **sustantivo, pronombre o verbo en gerundio (`-ing`)**; jamás se combinan directamente con una oración con verbo conjugado a menos que se use la fórmula *despite the fact that*:  
  *Despite the system failure, the failover worked.* / *In spite of having low bandwidth, the call continued.*
* **Whereas / While:** Contrastan dos realidades opuestas (*SATA SSDs use 6 Gbps lanes, whereas NVMe drives utilize PCIe buses*).

#### 2. Conectores de Adición (*Addition*)
* **Furthermore / Moreover / In addition / Additionally:** Introducen argumentos acumulativos con valor formal al inicio de oración seguidos de coma.
* **As well as / Besides:** Permiten la agregación directa de elementos o estructuras en `-ing` (*Besides monitoring CPU usage, the agent logs memory leaks*).

#### 3. Conectores de Causa y Efecto (*Cause & Effect*)
* **Because vs. Because of:**
    * `Because` + Cláusula con sujeto y verbo: *The server restarted because the power supply failed.*
    * `Because of` + Sustantivo / Sintagma nominal: *The server restarted because of a hardware failure.*
* **Therefore / Consequently / As a result / Hence / Thus:** Enlazan la consecuencia lógica deducida del enunciado previo (*The hard drive exceeded its read cycles; therefore, we replaced it immediately*).

#### 4. Conectores de Finalidad (*Purpose*)
* **In order to / So as to / To + Infinitivo:** Expresan el objetivo directo de una acción (*We rebooted the node in order to clear the memory buffers*). En forma negativa se formulan como `in order not to` o `so as not to`.
* **So that + Sujeto + Verbo Modal (can, could, would):** Se utiliza cuando el sujeto de la acción principal difiere del de la subordinada (*The admin configured a VLAN so that guest users could not access internal databases*).

<h2 style="color: #29b6f6;">3.6. Future Continuous, Future Perfect Simple & Future Perfect Continuous</h2>

Las formas aspectuales de futuro permiten posicionar cronológicamente eventos técnicos con alta precisión métrica:

### Matriz Comparativa de Tiempos de Futuro Compuestos

| Tiempo de Futuro | Estructura Sintáctica | Foco Comunicativo | Marcadores Temporales Típicos |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Future Continuous** | Sujeto + `will be` + Verbo-`-ing` | Acción que estará en **pleno desarrollo** en un instante temporal futuro acotado. | *At 10:00 tomorrow, this time next week, at midnight.* |
| **Future Perfect Simple** | Sujeto + `will have` + Participio de Pasado | Acción técnica que habrá **concluido de forma definitiva antes** de un límite futuro. | *By Friday, by the end of the month, by 18:00.* |
| **Future Perfect Continuous** | Sujeto + `will have been` + Verbo-`-ing` | **Cómputo de duración acumulada** ininterrumpida hasta alcanzar un punto futuro. | *By next July, for five years; by 20:00, for eight hours.* |

### Contraste Visual de los Aspectos de Futuro

```text
                                           FUTURE MILESTONE (By 22:00)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────│──────────────> Time
 FUTURE CONTINUOUS                                     │
 ════════════════════════════════════════════════════  │ (Acción en curso a las 22:00)
 "At 22:00, the IT staff will be migrating data."      │
                                                       │
 FUTURE PERFECT SIMPLE                                 │
 ──────────────● (Acción concluida antes del límite)   │
 "By 22:00, the IT staff will have migrated all data." │
                                                       │
 FUTURE PERFECT CONTINUOUS                             │
 ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════>│ (Mide la duración hasta las 22:00)
 "By 22:00, the server will have been running for 72 hours."
```

* *Future Continuous:* *"Please do not attempt to log in at 23:00 tonight, as the team **will be updating** the firewall firmware."*
* *Future Perfect Simple:* *"By the end of the quarter, the department **will have replaced** all obsolete mechanical hard drives."*
* *Future Perfect Continuous:* *"Next December, our lead systems engineer **will have been administering** this infrastructure for ten years."*

<h2 style="color: #29b6f6;">🎯 Practical Scenarios & Technical Application</h2>

!!! example "Scenario 1: Datacenter Scheduled Maintenance Notification"
    **Context:** Formal email notification sent to department heads before a maintenance window:  
    * *"Dear colleagues, please be advised that our secondary storage controller **is due to undergo** planned maintenance tonight. At 23:00, the infrastructure engineers **will be performing** parity checks on the RAID volume. By 04:00 tomorrow, we **will have restored** full write access to all network shares. Although some network latency may occur, the failover server **is to handle** all urgent database requests. We have **already** isolated the critical nodes, so no data loss will take place."*  
    * **Linguistic breakdown:** Periphrastic future (*is due to undergo, is to handle*); Future Continuous (*will be performing*); Future Perfect Simple with boundary marker (*By 04:00... will have restored*); connector of contrast (*Although*); temporal adverb (*already*).

!!! example "Scenario 2: Helpdesk Ticket Resolution & Troubleshooting"
    **Context:** Incident response recorded in the ticketing platform:  
    * *"The client's workstation was crashing repeatedly **because of** a corrupt graphics driver. The user was **still** attempting to run CAD applications **despite** multiple hardware warnings. We **are going to install** a certified driver package this afternoon. Consequently, the workstation will operate **smoothly**."*  
    * **Linguistic breakdown:** Causal connector with noun phrase (*because of*); temporal adverb (*still*); connector with noun (*despite*); intention based on prior analysis (*are going to install*); adverb of manner (*smoothly*).

<h2 style="color: #29b6f6;">📌 Technical Appendix: Exam Keys (Claves PAC)</h2>

!!! danger "Critical Grammar & Linguistic Rules for Assessment"
    1. **Future Forms Distinction Matrix:**
        * **Will:** Spontaneous decisions made at speech time (*"I'll check the router"*), predictions based on personal belief (*"I think it will work"*), promises, and offers.
        * **Be going to:** Pre-meditated plans/intentions (*"We are going to buy new SSDs"*), or predictions with observable present evidence (*"Look at the red LED; the drive is going to fail"*).
        * **Present Continuous:** Definite pre-arranged meetings with people or organizations (*"I am seeing the security auditor tomorrow at 10:00"*).
        * **Present Simple:** Fixed schedules, timetables, or calendarized events (*"The maintenance window starts at 22:00"*).
    2. **Future Perfect Disambiguation:**
        * The time preposition **`By...`** (*by Friday, by tomorrow, by 2030*) followed by a completed milestone demands **Future Perfect Simple** (`will have` + past participle).
        * If **`By...`** is paired with a duration phrase introduced by **`for...`** (*for three hours, for ten years*), the correct choice is **Future Perfect Continuous** (`will have been` + `-ing`).
    3. **Syntax of Periphrastic Futures (`Be due to`, `Be about to`, `Be to`):**
        * All three structures are strictly followed by an **infinitive without to** (base form):
            * ❌ *The train is about to leaving.* $\rightarrow$ ✔️ ***The train is about to leave.***
            * ❌ *The engineer is due to arriving at noon.* $\rightarrow$ ✔️ ***The engineer is due to arrive at noon.***
    4. **Contrast Linker Trap: *Despite / In spite of* vs. *Although / Even though*:**
        * **Although / Even though** must be followed by a complete clause (Subject + Finite Verb):  
          *Although the firewall was active, the port was scanned.*
        * **Despite / In spite of** must be followed by a noun, pronoun, or gerund (`-ing`):  
          *Despite the active firewall...* / *Despite having an active firewall...*  
          (❌ *Despite the firewall was active...* is grammatically incorrect).
    5. **Adjective vs. Adverb with Stative Verbs of Perception:**
        * Verbs of sensory perception (*taste, smell, look, sound, feel*) take an **adjective** (not an adverb) when describing the inherent state or quality of the subject:
            * ❌ *The replacement thermal paste smells strangely.* $\rightarrow$ ✔️ ***The replacement thermal paste smells strange.***
            * ❌ *The server rack looks stably.* $\rightarrow$ ✔️ ***The server rack looks stable.***
    6. **Temporal Adverbs Syntax (*Already, Still, Yet*):**
        * **Already:** Affirmatives (*"We have already deployed it"*).
        * **Still:** Continuance in affirmative/negative (*"The service is still loading"* / *"It still hasn't loaded"*).
        * **Yet:** Negatives and questions at clause end (*"Has the script finished yet?"* / *"It has not finished yet"*).
